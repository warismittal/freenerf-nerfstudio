\section{Failure analysis}
\textbf{Where vanilla NeRF fails at 8 views.} The per-run previews (\texttt{results/previews}) show the expected signature: renders from held-out poses keep the object's large structure but the expected-depth maps are full of isolated \emph{floaters} in empty space and a hazy shell around the object. The diagnostics quantify it: the share of rendering weight in the first 20 samples ($\Omega_M$) and the background opacity $B$ in Table~\ref{tab:ablation}. FreeNeRF's occlusion term targets exactly $\Omega_M$; the frequency mask targets high-frequency floaters.
\textbf{Where FreeNeRF does not help.} PSNR rewards getting the low frequencies right, LPIPS rewards sharp texture. Annealing the positional encoding removes high-frequency noise but also delays fine detail, so at a short training budget a PSNR gain need not come with an LPIPS gain; on Lego, LPIPS moves from \LegoALpips{} (vanilla) to \LegoDLpips{} (FreeNeRF). With \Iters{} iterations the mask is fully open only for the last 30\% of training; the paper trains much longer, so part of its LPIPS gain is likely a budget effect that we cannot test here.
\textbf{A collapse that is not about sparsity: Chair.} With the default white background, all three Chair runs, including the 100-view reference, converged to an \emph{empty} field: the training loss flattened at the value of predicting white everywhere by step $\sim$1.4k, and the renders were blank (identical 14.13\,dB for every arm). Chair covers about 16\% of each image, so ``everything is background'' is a strong local minimum for a ReLU MLP with a white background. Training Chair against a random background colour (ground-truth alpha kept, both predictions and targets blended with the same random colour) removes that minimum. The failed runs are kept in the repository as evidence; both arms of Chair share the fix, so the comparison stays paired.
\textbf{Self-captured scene.} COLMAP registered \RealRegistered{} photos, below the 95\% acceptance threshold we had set; the scene contains a glossy, nearly texture-less laptop screen, which gives SIFT few reliable matches. The scene also violates NeRF-synthetic's assumptions (unbounded clutter, a reflective screen, auto-exposure changes), so its absolute PSNR is not comparable with the synthetic scenes; only the paired vanilla-versus-FreeNeRF difference is meaningful.
