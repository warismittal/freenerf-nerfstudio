\section{Problem}
A neural radiance field (NeRF) \cite{mildenhall2020nerf} fits an MLP $F_\Theta:(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{d})\mapsto(\mathbf{c},\sigma)$ to posed images and renders a ray $\mathbf{r}$ by alpha compositing $K$ samples, $\hat{C}(\mathbf{r})=\sum_i T_i(1-e^{-\sigma_i\delta_i})\mathbf{c}_i$. With the 100 training views of a NeRF-synthetic scene it reaches above 30\,dB PSNR on held-out views; with 8 views it collapses: the MLP fits the 8 images with high-frequency ``floaters'' and background haze that are correct from the training cameras and wrong from everywhere else. Topic~16 asks us to implement one published regulariser, report PSNR and LPIPS at 8 views on Lego, Chair and one self-captured scene for vanilla and regularised NeRF, render novel-view videos, and \emph{quantify how much of the 100-to-8-view gap the regulariser recovers}. We use FreeNeRF \cite{yang2023freenerf}, the simplest strong baseline (no extra network, no pre-training), and nerfstudio's \texttt{vanilla-nerf} \cite{tancik2023nerfstudio} as the base. We measure recovery with the gap-recovery ratio
\begin{equation}
\rho=\frac{\mathrm{PSNR}_\text{reg}(8)-\mathrm{PSNR}_\text{van}(8)}{\mathrm{PSNR}_\text{van}(100)-\mathrm{PSNR}_\text{van}(8)},
\label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}
where $\rho=0$ means no help and $\rho=1$ means the 8-view model matches the 100-view one, with a 95\% interval from a paired bootstrap over test views and seeds.

\section{Related work}
\textbf{Sparse-view NeRF.} Methods either learn a prior from many scenes or regularise a single scene. pixelNeRF \cite{yu2021pixelnerf} conditions the field on image features learned across scenes. Per-scene methods add a loss or a schedule: DietNeRF \cite{jain2021dietnerf} matches CLIP features of rendered and training views and introduced the 8-view Blender protocol we follow; DS-NeRF \cite{deng2022dsnerf} supervises depth with sparse SfM points; RegNeRF \cite{niemeyer2022regnerf} renders patches from unobserved poses and penalises depth roughness; DiffusioNeRF \cite{wynn2023diffusionerf} replaces the hand-made prior by a learned diffusion prior on RGB-D patches. FSGS \cite{zhu2024fsgs} moves the problem to Gaussian splatting.
\textbf{Frequency and positional encoding.} Fourier features \cite{tancik2020fourier} explain why NeRF can fit fine detail and why it overfits when supervision is sparse. BARF \cite{lin2021barf} anneals the encoding bands coarse-to-fine to make pose estimation well-posed; FreeNeRF \cite{yang2023freenerf} applies the same idea to few-shot training and adds an occlusion penalty on density near the camera, reporting 13.93$\to$24.26\,dB at 8 views on Blender over a JAX mip-NeRF base.
\textbf{Metrics.} We report PSNR, SSIM \cite{wang2004ssim} and LPIPS (AlexNet) \cite{zhang2018lpips}, all computed by our own evaluation module from exported renders.
