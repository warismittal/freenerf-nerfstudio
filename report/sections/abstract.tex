\begin{abstract}
NeRF trained on 8 views instead of 100 fits its training images but fails from new viewpoints. We re-implement FreeNeRF, a frequency-annealed positional encoding plus a near-camera occlusion penalty, as a nerfstudio method and measure how much of the 100-to-8-view gap it recovers, with paired bootstrap intervals, a $2\times2$ ablation of its two terms, a RegNeRF comparator, and a self-captured scene posed with COLMAP. On Lego at 8 views FreeNeRF changes PSNR from \LegoAPsnr{} to \LegoDPsnr\,dB, recovering $\rho=\LegoRho$ of the gap to the 100-view model (\LegoGap\,dB). The pre-declared primary hypothesis is \HTwoStatus. Every number is reproducible with one command from logged, seeded runs on a free Kaggle T4 budget.
\end{abstract}
